# Relational Geometry — Failed Approaches

This document records approaches that have been explored and found insufficient, together with the mathematical lesson extracted from each failure.

---

## Failure 1 — Fixed Boolean Relational States as an Oscillator

### Attempt

Represent arguments using Boolean values and attempt to generate an oscillator entirely from fixed relations among those arguments.

### Result

The system tends toward static consistency.

Once all relations are simultaneously enforced, the detector becomes overdetermined and subsequent states are uniquely fixed or frozen.

### Lesson

A nontrivial oscillator requires some form of incomplete realization.

A system cannot merely contain two states and a set of static constraints; there must be a mechanism by which the detector does not represent the full relational configuration at every instant.

### Consequence

Investigate partial projection and shadow.

---

## Failure 2 — Primitive Transition Relation

### Attempt

Introduce a relation explicitly requiring an argument to change state on the next detector iteration.

### Result

This produces the desired dynamics but at the cost of inserting the desired phenomenon as a primitive.

### Lesson

The transition must emerge from the existing relational grammar.

### Consequence

Do not introduce primitive transition unless all emergent alternatives fail.

---

## Failure 3 — Arbitrary Detector Permeability

### Attempt

Allow a detector to admit new relations from Being whenever those relations are compatible with existing relations.

### Result

A detector can expand toward arbitrary relational structure.

In particular, a detector class intended to represent non-simultaneous structure may eventually admit simultaneous relations simply because those relations are present in Being.

### Lesson

Detector permeability must be argument-bounded.

### Consequence

Admissibility must depend on available or relationally instantiable arguments.

---

## Failure 4 — Treating Non-Simultaneity as Exclusion

### Attempt

Represent non-simultaneous relations using the same formal operation as exclusive relations.

### Result

This collapses two conceptually distinct phenomena:

- incompatibility within one detector state;
- separation across detector succession.

### Lesson

Non-simultaneity is inclusive at the relational level even though its arguments are not simultaneously realized.

### Consequence

Maintain separate primitives for exclusion and non-simultaneity.

---

## Failure 5 — Treating Existence as Presence

### Attempt

Define existence as simply being represented in a detector.

### Result

Existence becomes indistinguishable from instantaneous detection.

It cannot express persistence, oscillation, or changing relational form.

### Lesson

Existence must be defined over detector succession.

### Consequence

Use persistence under detection as the formal existence criterion.

---

## Failure 6 — Assuming Form Must Persist for Existence

### Attempt

Require an entity to maintain the same relational state in order to continue existing.

### Result

Changing entities cannot exist.

Oscillators become impossible by definition.

### Lesson

Existence must not entail invariant form.

### Consequence

Define existence using an identity criterion weaker than state invariance.

---

## Failure 7 — Smuggling Time into the Detector

### Attempt

Treat the detector itself as an event with duration.

### Result

The detector becomes an ordinary temporal process rather than the infinitesimally thin relational operation the theory requires.

### Lesson

The detector should have no duration.

### Consequence

Put succession between detector realizations rather than inside a detector.

---

## Failure 8 — Assuming Complex Numbers Too Early

### Attempt

Use complex amplitudes and Euler rotation immediately to produce oscillation.

### Result

The desired mathematics can be reproduced, but it is unclear whether anything has been derived.

### Lesson

Complex amplitudes should be treated as a possible recovered representation.

### Consequence

First construct the relational projection structure independently and then determine whether complex analysis naturally represents it.
