# Relational Geometry
A research program investigating whether a geometry of relations can generate detector states, persistence, dynamics, probability, and causality without taking those phenomena as primitive.
## Core conceptual sequence
$$
\boxed{
\text{Being}
\rightarrow
\text{Relations}
\rightarrow
\text{Arguments}
\rightarrow
\text{Admissibility}
\rightarrow
\text{Detection}
\rightarrow
\text{Succession}
\rightarrow
\text{Persistence}
\rightarrow
\text{Existence}
}
$$
The current research extension investigates:
$$
\boxed{
\text{Multidimensional Relations}
\rightarrow
\text{Projection}
\rightarrow
\text{Shadow}
\rightarrow
\text{Partial Projection}
\rightarrow
\text{Dynamics}
}
$$

## Epistemic discipline
RG distinguishes:
- definitions;
- axioms;
- lemmas;
- theorems;
- conjectures;
- computational results;
- interpretations;
- failed approaches.
A conjecture must never silently become an axiom.
A computational result must never silently become a proof.
A successful proof must not be obtained by weakening the theory merely to make the desired result true.
## Immediate objective
Construct the smallest relational system exhibiting a recurrent detector sequence through partial projection and shadow, without primitive transition or causality.
## Formalization objective
Determine the algebraic structure underlying:
$$
R,\quad A,\quad D,\quad \mathcal A(D),\quad \pi,\quad \sigma
$$
and formalize the resulting theory in Lean.
## Long-term objective
Determine whether familiar mathematical structures associated with dynamics, probability, wave functions, and causality can be recovered as consequences or representations of relational geometry rather than introduced as primitives.
