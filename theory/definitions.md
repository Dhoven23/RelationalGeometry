Relational Geometry — Definitions

Status: Working terminology
Last updated: 2026-09-01

⸻

Being

The total relational structure from which detector-level realization is derived.

Being contains relations but no primitive arguments.

⸻

Relation

A primitive structural element of Being.

A relation constrains arguments when realized within a detector.

Relations possess primitive is-ness.

⸻

Argument

A detector-level entity instantiated through relational structure.

An argument does not possess primitive independent determination.

⸻

Is-ness

Participation in Being.

Relations possess primitive is-ness.

Arguments possess secondary is-ness through participation in relations.

Is-ness is conceptually prior to formal existence.

⸻

Existence

Persistence under detector succession.

An entity exists in a detector when incrementing the detector does not destroy the entity under the relevant identity criterion.

Existence therefore does not imply unchanged state or unchanged relational form.

⸻

Detector

An infinitesimally thin relational structure that determines what is and is not the case.

The detector has no duration.

⸻

Detector State

The set of arguments and relations realized as the case within a detector instance.

⸻

Detector Succession

A sequence of detector realizations:

$$
D_0,D_1,D_2,\ldots
$$

in which the output of one detection constrains the admissibility conditions of subsequent detection.

⸻

Potential State Space

The set of potential states available to the arguments of a detector.

Denoted:

$$
\Omega(D).
$$

⸻

Admissibility

The relation between potential states and the relational constraints under which those states may be realized.

The admissible state space is denoted:

$$
\mathcal A(D)\subseteq\Omega(D).
$$

⸻

Detection

The operation by which admissible states are selected from potential states and unsupported relations are pruned.

Schematically:

$$
D:\Omega(D)\rightarrow\mathcal A(D).
$$

⸻

Pruning

The removal of a relation from a detector when no admissible argument remains for that relation to constrain.

⸻

Self-Relation

A relation of an argument to itself.

$$
A\leftrightarrow A.
$$

Self-relation provides the minimal condition for participation in detector realization.

⸻

Other-Relation

A relation connecting an argument to something other than itself.

Other-relations provide relational determination beyond self-relation.

⸻

Implicit Relation

The relation of an argument to what else is the case.

Every argument that is the case is required to possess both self-relation and an implicit other-relation.

⸻

Inclusive Relation

A relation permitting its arguments to be simultaneously admissible within a detector state.

⸻

Exclusive Relation

A relation making its arguments mutually incompatible within a detector state.

⸻

Simultaneous Relation

An inclusive relation whose arguments are asserted to be the case in the same infinitesimal detector instance.

⸻

Non-Simultaneous Relation

An inclusive relation whose arguments are related across distinct detector instances.

Non-simultaneity is not equivalent to exclusion.

⸻

Relational Subset

A subset of the relational structure of Being admitted by a detector because it is compatible with the detector's existing relations and can instantiate or constrain available arguments.

⸻

Projection

A mapping from a multidimensional relational structure into the dimensions represented by a detector.

⸻

Shadow

The relational component of a structure that is constrained by the detector but is not represented in the detector's visible state.

The mathematical definition of shadow remains provisional.

⸻

Partial Projection

A state in which only part of a relational configuration is represented in the detector.

Symbolically:

$$
0<|\pi(r)|<|r|.
$$

⸻

Cancellation

A state in which the relevant relational structure has no detector-visible projection.

Cancellation does not necessarily imply destruction of the underlying relational structure.

⸻

Oscillator

A detector succession containing at least two distinct states that recur sequentially without external intervention on the detector.

A minimal oscillator therefore requires:

$$
D_i\neq D_{i+1}
$$

and eventual recurrence:

$$
D_i=D_{i+n}
$$

for some $`n>1`$, under an appropriate state-equivalence criterion.

⸻

Closure

A mathematical operation that determines whether applying the relevant relational constraints to a state produces any further admissible structure.

The exact closure operator for RG has not yet been defined.

⸻

Relational Dimension

The number of independent degrees of relational structure required to specify a relational configuration.

The concept is currently heuristic and requires formal definition.

⸻

Dimensional Mismatch

A condition in which the relational structure constraining a detector possesses more degrees of freedom than the detector represents.

This is a candidate mechanism for partial projection and apparent indeterminacy.
