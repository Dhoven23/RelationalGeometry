# Relational Geometry — Research Decisions

---

## 2026-08-31 — Existence Is Downstream

**Decision**

Existence should not be primitive.

Define existence as persistence under detector succession.

\[
\text{existence}
=
\text{persistence under detection}.
\]

**Reason**

This avoids requiring primitive object permanence and allows entities to change relational configuration while continuing to exist.

---

## 2026-08-31 — Relations and Arguments Are Pre-Existence

**Decision**

Do not define relations or arguments as primitive entities satisfying the predicate "exists."

Instead distinguish:

- Being
- is-ness
- existence

Relations possess primitive is-ness.

Arguments receive secondary is-ness through relational participation.

Existence is introduced only after detection and succession.

---

## 2026-08-31 — Arguments Do Not Self-Exist

**Decision**

Arguments are not primitive constituents of Being.

They are instantiated through relations.

**Reason**

This removes the need to decide whether relations or things are metaphysically prior by simply defining arguments as detector-level relational realizations.

---

## 2026-08-31 — Constraint Must Be Invertible

**Decision**

The statement "arguments are constrained by relations" must admit an inverse:

\[
\text{no argument}
\Rightarrow
\text{no active relation}.
\]

**Reason**

Otherwise the detector becomes arbitrarily permeable to relations from Being.

This was identified as a major flaw in the earlier detector formulation.

---

## 2026-08-31 — Detector Is Not Arbitrarily Permeable

**Decision**

A detector may admit a new relation only when that relation has available or relationally instantiable arguments.

**Reason**

Without this restriction, a non-simultaneous detector could expand indiscriminately toward all of Being and eventually acquire arbitrary simultaneous relations.

---

## 2026-08-31 — Being Contains No Arguments

**Decision**

Being contains relations but no primitive arguments.

Arguments can come into detector existence when admissible relations instantiate them.

**Consequence**

A detector cannot simply "discover" arbitrary arguments.

New arguments must arise through relational structure already available to the detector.

---

## 2026-08-31 — Non-Simultaneous Is Not Exclusive

**Decision**

Non-simultaneous relation is distinct from exclusive relation.

Exclusive:

\[
A\mid B
\]

means incompatibility within one detector state.

Non-simultaneous:

\[
A\mathrel{N}B
\]

means relational separation across detector succession.

---

## 2026-08-31 — No Primitive Causality

**Decision**

Do not introduce causality as a primitive.

The desired primitive ingredients are:

\[
\text{simultaneity}
+
\text{admissibility}.
\]

Apparent causation should emerge from detector succession.

---

## 2026-08-31 — No Primitive Transition Relation

**Decision**

Do not introduce a primitive "transition" relation merely to force an oscillator.

**Reason**

A transition relation risks smuggling dynamics into the theory.

Instead seek a mechanism in which transition emerges from incomplete projection, admissibility, and detector succession.

---

## 2026-08-31 — Projection Is Necessary for Dynamics

**Decision**

The original Boolean formulation is insufficient as a likely source of nontrivial oscillation.

Introduce multidimensional relational structure and detector projection as the leading candidate mechanism.

**Reason**

A fully specified Boolean detector state tends toward overdetermination.

Partial projection allows a relational configuration to be constrained without being fully represented.

---

## 2026-08-31 — Shadow Is Not Nonexistence

**Decision**

A detector-invisible relational component should not automatically be treated as nonexistent.

The current hypothesis distinguishes:

\[
\text{not represented}
\]

from:

\[
\text{not present in relational structure}.
\]

---

## 2026-09-01 — Oscillation as Projection Cycle

**Working hypothesis**

A minimal oscillator may be a cycle of:

\[
\text{full projection}
\rightarrow
\text{partial projection}
\rightarrow
\text{shadow/cancellation}
\rightarrow
\text{partial projection}
\rightarrow
\text{full projection}.
\]

This replaces the earlier attempt to make fixed Boolean states oscillate.

---

## 2026-09-01 — Boolean Algebra Is Probably Insufficient

**Decision**

Boolean algebra remains useful for the minimal admissibility toy model but should not be assumed to provide the eventual relational geometry.

The desired formalism must represent at least:

- relational structure;
- projection;
- hidden/shadow degrees of freedom;
- partial realization;
- detector-visible state.

---

## 2026-09-01 — Euler/Complex Representation Is a Candidate Representation

Complex amplitudes and Euler's formula may eventually provide a convenient representation of projection/rotation.

They are not currently primitive.

The desired direction is to determine whether complex analysis can be **recovered** from the relational structure rather than assumed at the beginning.
